Christmas party planning guide

How to Host a Christmas Party with Printable Games

Pick 2–3 printable games, print them the night before, and run them in this order: a talking game first, a team game in the middle, a singing game last. That is the whole plan — the rest of this page is the timeline, the printing checklist, and which bundle fits which crowd.

The evening, hour by hour

A Christmas party does not need a schedule pinned to the wall — it needs a shape. This one works for a family party of about two hours, and it scales up to an office party by splitting guests into teams.

Before the party: print the night before

  • Print one game sheet per player — or one per team for a big group.
  • Cut out the charades cards and bingo calling cards — fold the calling cards and drop them in a bowl.
  • Keep one answer key for the host. Players get game sheets only.
  • Have pens, scissors and small prizes ready — candy canes, ornaments, or a silly trophy for the bingo winner.

First 15 minutes: a talking game

Start with Would You Rather, Christmas Edition — it needs no explaining and gets everyone talking. Read each pair aloud, everyone picks A or B and says why, and the funniest reason wins the round. It is in the Christmas Party Games Bundle (8 games, 12 pages, $6), and the free sample on free printables is the same game, so you can test it tonight.

Middle: a team game

Play Family Feud or Scattergories in teams of 4–6 — nobody waits long for a turn, and mixed ages help because every team can answer something. Bingo also works here as a warm-up: the Christmas Printables Mega Pack (18 pages, $7) has 4 bingo cards and 30 calling cards, plus trivia for after dinner.

Last: a singing game

End on Finish the Carol — everyone sings, nobody loses. It is the gentlest way to close the evening, and the answer key stays with the host until the end.

For the kids, all evening

Keep colouring pages, a word search and a scavenger hunt on a side table. Kids drift in and out of the main games; the quiet activities keep them happy while the adults talk. All three are in the Mega Pack.

How many games do you need?

Two or three games per party is enough. A short, lively session beats a long, tired one — pick one talking game, one team game and one closer, and leave the rest in the pack for next year. The packs are reusable: print them again every Christmas.

Printing checklist

  • Print at 100% / "Actual size" — never "Fit to page", or the cards come out the wrong size.
  • US Letter paper (8.5 × 11 in); the PDFs also print fine on A4 at 100%.
  • Card stock for bingo cards, charades cards and gift tags — they survive the party.
  • Plain paper for everything else.
  • Test-print one page first if your printer is new to you.

Questions

How many printable games do I need for a Christmas party?

Two or three games is enough for a one- to two-hour party: one talking game to start, one team game in the middle, and one gentle closer such as Finish the Carol.

What should I print the night before?

One game sheet per player (or per team), the charades and bingo calling cards cut out, and one answer key for the host. Print at 100% scale on US Letter paper, and use card stock for the cards.
