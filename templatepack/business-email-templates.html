Payment Reminder — Polite

Use this when: the first chase for a late invoice — friendly, not awkward

The firm and final payment reminders are in the Pro Pack.

Subject: Friendly reminder: invoice [invoice number] due [due date]

Hi [Name],

I hope you are well. This is a friendly reminder that invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was due on [due date].

If you have already sent payment, please ignore this email — and thank you. If not, you can pay by [payment method/link] at your convenience.

If there is a problem with the invoice, or you need a copy, just reply to this email and I will sort it out.

Many thanks,
[Your name]
[Company] | [Phone]

Out-of-Office Email

Use this when: before a holiday or any time away from email

Subject: Out of office until [return date]

Hello,

Thank you for your email. I am out of the office until [return date] and will not be checking email regularly.

For urgent matters, please contact [colleague's name] at [colleague's email]. For anything else, I will reply when I am back on [return date].

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
[Your name]

Resignation Letter

Use this when: when you need to resign and want the wording right

Subject: Resignation — [Your name], effective [last working day]

Dear [Manager's name],

Please accept this letter as formal notice of my resignation from my role as [job title] at [company], effective [last working day, giving the notice period in your contract].

I have enjoyed working with the team, and I am grateful for the opportunities I have had here. I will do everything I can to hand over my work smoothly, including [handover tasks].

Thank you for your support. I wish you and the team well.

Yours sincerely,
[Your name]
[Address] | [Phone] | [Email]

Apology Email to a Client

Use this when: when something went wrong and you need to put it right

Subject: Apology — [what went wrong]

Dear [Name],

I am sorry about [what happened, in plain words]. I understand this caused [the effect on the client], and that is not the standard we aim for.

Here is what happened: [one or two sentences of explanation, no excuses]. Here is what we are doing about it: [the fix, and when it will be done].

To make this right, we would like to [offer, e.g. redo the work, extend the deadline, apply a credit]. Please let me know if that works for you, or if there is something else you would prefer.

Thank you for your patience, and for giving us the chance to put this right.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
[Job title] | [Company] | [Phone]

Introduction / Referral Email

Use this when: when you are introducing two people who should know each other

Subject: Introducing [Name A] and [Name B]

Hi [Name A] and [Name B],

I would like to introduce you. [Name A], [one line about who they are and what they do]. [Name B], [one line about who they are and what they do].

I think you should talk because [the reason — a shared interest, a problem one can solve for the other].

I will leave it with you to connect directly. [Name A], [Name B]'s email is [email]. [Name B], [Name A]'s email is [email].

Best wishes,
[Your name]

Price Increase Notice

Use this when: when your prices are going up and clients need to know first

Subject: Changes to our pricing from [date]

Dear [Name],

I am writing to let you know that our prices for [product/service] will change from [date]. [Product/service] will move from [old price] to [new price].

We have held our prices since [date], but [one-line reason, e.g. “our costs have risen sharply”]. We have kept the increase as small as we could.

Your current pricing stays in place until [date]. If you have any questions, or would like to discuss your plan, please reply to this email or call me on [phone].

Thank you for your business.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Company]

Good to know

  • Keep the subject specific — “Invoice 1042 due Friday” beats “Quick question”.
  • Put the ask in the first two lines. Most people read the first two lines and decide.
  • Never send a price increase on a Friday — give people the working week to reply. (That is a tip, not a rule.)
  • Save the version you sent, so you know exactly what the other person received.
