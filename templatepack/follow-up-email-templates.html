Follow-Up After a Meeting

Use this when: after any meeting, to confirm what was agreed

Subject: Following up on our meeting on [date]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for your time today. It was good to discuss [topic], and I appreciated your thoughts on [point they raised].

As agreed, I will [your next step] by [date]. On your side, the next step is [their next step]. Please let me know if I have missed anything.

I have attached [document, if any]. If it would help, I am happy to meet again next week — just let me know a time that suits you.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Your job title] | [Company] | [Phone]

Follow-Up After No Reply

Use this when: when someone has not replied after 3–5 working days

Subject: Checking in — [original subject]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to follow up on my email from [date] about [topic]. I know inboxes get busy, so I am keeping this short.

The one thing I need from you is [the ask, in one sentence]. If now is not a good time, just let me know when would be better.

If someone else on your team is the right person for this, please feel free to forward this email to them.

Thanks for your time,
[Your name]

Thank-You Email After an Interview

Use this when: within 24 hours of a job interview

Subject: Thank you — [job title] interview

Dear [Interviewer's name],

Thank you for taking the time to meet me today about the [job title] role. I enjoyed learning more about [something specific from the interview, e.g. the team's plans for the year].

Our conversation confirmed my interest in the role. In particular, I was glad to hear about [detail], because [why it fits your experience].

Please let me know if you need anything else from me. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Phone] | [Email]

Follow-Up After Sending a Proposal

Use this when: a few days after sending a proposal or quote

Subject: Following up — proposal for [project]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to follow up on the proposal I sent on [date] for [project or service].

The short version: [one sentence — what you proposed and what it costs]. I am happy to walk through any part of it, or to adjust the scope if your priorities have changed.

Would a short call this week help? I am free on [option 1] or [option 2]. If the timing is no longer right, just let me know and I will close the file for now.

Best regards,
[Your name]

Follow-Up After a Call

Use this when: after a phone call, to confirm the next steps

Subject: Good to speak — next steps from our call

Hi [Name],

Thank you for the call today. It was good to talk through [topic].

To confirm what we agreed: [point 1]; [point 2]. I will [your next step] by [date], and you will [their next step] by [date].

I have attached [document, if any]. If I have missed anything, please let me know.

Best regards,
[Your name]

Thank-You Email

Use this when: whenever someone helped you and a thank-you is due

Subject: Thank you

Hi [Name],

Thank you for [what they did — your time, your help, the introduction, the feedback].

It made a real difference because [one sentence — what changed as a result]. I appreciate it.

I hope we can [work together again / stay in touch]. Please let me know if there is ever anything I can do for you.

With thanks,
[Your name]

Good to know

  • Follow up within 24 hours of a meeting or interview.
  • One ask per email — if you need three things, send three short emails, not one long one.
  • Keep it under 150 words. Short follow-ups get answered; long ones get saved for later.
  • If there is still no reply, send the “after no reply” version once, then stop.

Questions

How soon should I follow up?

Within 24 hours of a meeting or interview, while the conversation is fresh. After no reply, wait three to five working days before following up once.

Can I send the same template twice?

You can follow up once after no reply. If there is still no answer, stop — a second chase rarely helps and can annoy the other person.

Are these OK for job applications?

Yes. The thank-you after an interview and the follow-up after no reply work for job applications — just keep them short and specific to the role.

Do I need to change much?

Only the [bracketed] parts: names, dates, the topic, and the one thing you are asking for. The rest is ready to send.
