Snowball vs avalanche — which one actually works?

There are two well-known ways to pay off several debts. The avalanche method attacks the debt with the highest interest rate first, because that is the debt costing you the most. The snowball method attacks the smallest balance first, because paying a debt off completely feels like progress — and feeling like you are making progress is what keeps people paying. On the numbers alone, avalanche always wins: it pays the least total interest. On a $10,000 debt at 20% and a $5,000 debt at 18%, avalanche saves a few hundred dollars over the life of both debts compared with snowball.

But the numbers are not the whole story. The snowball method exists because the hardest part of paying off debt is not the maths, it is the months in the middle when nothing seems to change. Clearing the smallest debt first gives you a win early — one account closed, one payment freed up, one less thing to think about — and that win is often what keeps people going through the long stretch. Research on how people actually behave with debt has repeatedly found that focusing on small balances first keeps people paying longer, even though it costs more in interest. The honest answer is: avalanche is cheaper, snowball is easier to stick to, and the best method is the one you will still be following in a year.

Whatever method you pick, two rules matter more than the method. First, keep paying the same total every month: when one debt is gone, roll its payment into the next debt instead of spending it. That is how the payoff accelerates — the same money, applied to fewer and fewer debts. Second, stop adding to the balances. A payoff plan that assumes no new charges falls apart the moment a new charge appears, and the calculator's date assumes the APR never changes and you never miss a payment. If your payment does not even cover the monthly interest, the balance grows instead of shrinking, and the calculator will tell you so — that is the moment to call the lender, not to pay less.

Questions, answered

What is the snowball method?

Pay off the smallest balance first, whatever its interest rate. When it is gone, roll its payment into the next smallest. It costs slightly more interest, but the early wins help people stick with it.

What is the avalanche method?

Pay off the highest APR first, whatever its balance. It pays the least total interest, because the most expensive debt is attacked first.

Why does my payment need to cover the interest?

If the monthly payment is smaller than the interest charged that month, the balance grows even while you pay. The calculator warns you when that is the case — increase the payment or talk to the lender.

What is not included?

New charges, late fees, changes to the APR, and balance-transfer offers. The estimate assumes a fixed APR, no new charges and no missed payments.
