Where your paycheque goes

A salary is not what you take home. Before the money reaches your bank account, four things come out of it: federal income tax, state income tax (in most states), Social Security and Medicare. On an $80,000 salary for a single filer in California, those four take about $18,357 a year, leaving about $61,643 — roughly 77% of the salary. The same $80,000 in Texas leaves about $65,110, because Texas has no state income tax. The difference is the state line, and it is the part of the paycheque most people forget when they compare job offers in different states.

Federal tax is worked out in brackets: the first slice of taxable income is taxed at 10%, the next at 12%, and so on up to 37%. Only the income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate — a common misunderstanding is that moving into a higher bracket taxes all of your income at the higher rate, which is not true. Before the brackets are applied, the standard deduction is subtracted: $16,100 for single filers in 2026, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly, $24,150 for heads of household. So on $80,000, a single filer's taxable income is $63,900, and the federal tax comes to $8,770.

Social Security takes 6.2% of pay, but only up to the wage base — $184,500 in 2026 — so the most anyone pays is $11,439 a year. Medicare takes 1.45% of all pay, with an extra 0.9% on pay above $200,000 for single filers ($250,000 for married couples). State tax is where this calculator is deliberately simplified: California and New York both have many brackets and their own deductions, and this calculator uses their published brackets without the many credits and adjustments a real return would include. Treat the state line as a planning estimate, not a tax return. And remember what is missing entirely: 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums, and any local or city tax — all of which come out of a real paycheque before it reaches you.

Questions, answered

Why is my take-home pay less than my salary?

Federal tax, state tax, Social Security and Medicare are all withheld before the money reaches you. On a typical salary they take roughly a fifth to a quarter of gross pay.

Does moving into a higher tax bracket tax all my income more?

No. Only the income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. A pay rise can never leave you with less take-home pay because of the brackets alone.

Why only five states?

Each state has its own tax rules. California and New York are the two with the most complex income tax; Texas, Florida and Washington have no state income tax, so the calculator shows no state-tax line for them. More states may be added later.

What is not included?

Pre-tax deductions such as 401(k) contributions and health insurance premiums, local or city taxes, and any state credits or adjustments. Your real paycheque will be lower than this estimate if you have those deductions.
